How it works

Learn the Mass one card at a time

Every card works the same way, so there is nothing to learn but the Latin.

  1. I

    Hear it

    Tap play and listen. The voice is AI-generated, not a recording of a person, and it follows Roman pronunciation, so each word sounds as it does at Mass.

  2. II

    Read it

    Turn the card over. The Latin sits with its English and a picture to hold it in mind, as in a hand missal.

  3. III

    Remember it

    Say how well you knew it: Again, Hard, Good or Easy. The app brings the card back just before you would forget. A few minutes a day.

Common questions

Frequently asked

If you have a question that is not here, email info@thelatinmass.org and it will probably make it onto this list.

What is The Latin Mass App?
A spaced-repetition flashcard app for memorising the 1962 Tridentine Latin Mass. It contains 643 root words and 83 phrase pairs drawn from the Order of Mass and selected Propers, with clear Latin audio on every card (AI-generated with ElevenLabs, with Roman ecclesiastical pronunciation rules applied) and a devotional image.
How many cards does it include?
643 root words and 83 phrase pairs covering the Order of Mass and selected Propers from the 1962 Missale Romanum. Cards cover nouns, verbs, adjectives, and complete liturgical phrases with English translations.
What scheduling algorithm does it use?
FSRS (the Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler), an open-source method that works out when you are about to forget each card and brings it back just before that. Anki now offers FSRS as a built-in option too. You do not need to know any of this to use the app. The Again, Hard, Good and Easy buttons do the work for you.
What does it cost?
Free during the beta. After beta, a small annual subscription will fund the project; the revenue after Stripe fees and minimal operating costs is split 50/50 between two Brisbane Latin Mass parishes. A public transparency ledger of every disbursement will be published when subscriptions go live.
Who is it for?
Lay parishioners who want to follow the Mass in Latin, choristers and schola directors learning the propers, seminarians preparing for ordination, and clergy refreshing their working Latin. The interlinear English layout works at any level.
Do I need Anki or any other tool?
No. The app runs in any modern browser, including on a phone, and stores your progress in the cloud so you can pick up where you left off on any device.
Is the audio AI or a real voice?
AI-generated, with ElevenLabs. No person recorded these cards. Each one is made with an Italian voice, with Roman (ecclesiastical) pronunciation rules applied to the Latin, so c, g, ti and ae sound as they do at Mass. What that gives you is a clear voice that says every word the same way each time. What it does not give you is a priest or a cantor, and we would rather say so plainly.
What is coming next?
Currently in beta: 1962 Order of Mass + selected Propers. Roadmap (subject to change): the full annual Proper cycle, sung-tone audio variants, custom decks for individual scholas, and a printable phrase booklet companion. Beta users help set priorities.
